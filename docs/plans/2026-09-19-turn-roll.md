# Whole-body turn roll

Goal: the explorer visibly rolls its whole body into turns, like a game flying hero. The chase camera mostly sees the back, so a turn should read as the figure banking into it, not only as the limbs and torso carving.

Owner decision (2026-09-18): the roll is part of the flight work, landing as its own change after the authored flight clips (PR #8, live 2026-09-19).

Finding: keyboard turns drive `pose.bank` to only about .05 of its ±.3 range, because body yaw is held near the view yaw and the bank is derived from the lag between them.

Design, chosen by two independent designs and an adversarial judge:
- A suit-layer module (`src/world/suitRoll.ts`) reads the lateral acceleration of the actual travel, gathered per physics step (`src/game/turnSweep.ts`) without the turns the flight safety makes, eased and soft-saturated, and faded in with speed. The presentation pose, the camera and physics are unchanged; the value is shared as a plain number (`presentation.suitRoll`) for telemetry only.
- The roll turns the model about the camera sight line, weighted by how directly the camera sees the flight axis. A rotation about the line to the camera cannot change any body direction's dot product with that line, so the back-to-camera guarantee holds by construction.
- Peak roll .8 rad (about 46°) with hero poses, .55 without. Reduced motion removes it. It fades out on touchdown and during the landing flare, and gives no roll when flying backward.
- The old long-axis bank term is removed; the hover side tilt stays and fades out as the roll fades in.
