# Flight study decisions

- Flight uses original authored clips on the 21-bone skeleton. Clips are typed keyframe tables (`src/world/flightClips.ts`, `flightAccents.ts`) compiled to quaternions and sampled by a purpose-built C1 sampler (`clipSampler.ts`), not three's AnimationMixer, whose write-skipping, bind-pose fill and shortest-arc blending conflict with the procedural layer. Blends are sign-aligned to per-bone pivots so wide arm swings pass in front of the face. Speed selects hover, cruise and power; the hero fist deploys with hysteresis (up at 20 m/s, down at 15 m/s) and is aimed along the travel axis; turns carve from the lateral acceleration of the travel, with the fist steering from the commanded heading slightly ahead of the body; braking hunches so the back stays toward an overhead camera. Reduced camera motion keeps every pose at full strength and damps only the motion (loops to 30%, no carve or steer, launch to 30%). The clips blend over what the pose targets wrote each frame, weighted by the flight weight, and the living layer adds its motion afterwards; only its hover drift and idle arms give way to the authored pose. The landing flare carries through touchdown and eases out over .45 s. The ground keeps the procedural layer and is bit-identical once the flight weight is zero; clips never enter `suit.glb`. Poses are sized to a silhouette floor: from the chase camera each flight state must move a hand tip and a toe tip at least 15 cm on screen (at the explorer's depth) away from the clips-off pose, checked by `tests/flight-silhouette.test.ts`. The landing flare is the one exception (.075 m for the feet): its legs keep the grounded stance so touchdown does not scissor. Each state also keeps its own silhouette: key pairs (hover and cruise, cruise and climb, cruise and dive, hover and the flare, classic and hero power) must differ by at least .3 m at some tip, measured on the pose alone, and `tests/flight-read.test.ts` checks the reads in the chase image (arm angles, leg spread, the hero fist's distance from the head). Climb and dive are whole clips on the limbs (the dive also on the back), not capped accents. In cruise the arms sweep back ahead of the legs (full by P = .2). Turns keep the arms close and the legs together (at power speed the legs keep the straight line) and leave the read to the whole-body roll. The brake's arm reach scales with the recent top speed, so a stop out of a slow drift keeps the hover arms. After touchdown the clips keep authority over the arms and ease it out over .45 s, and the living layer's idle arms and impact reaction give way by the same amount, so they never stack on the flare or fling the arms out of the cruise sweep; while the legs hand back after a touchdown, each foot (then the toe) lifts by what its thigh and shin still pitch back, so a knee folded in flight does not tip the toes into the ground.
- The explorer's skeleton has 21 bones so that authored clips can bend the spine, set the head on the neck, shrug the shoulders, turn the wrists and point the feet. The ten legacy joints keep their indices and exact pivots, so the pose and living-motion code is unchanged. Each added bone rests at identity, descends from the legacy joint it grew out of, and takes its skin weights from that joint; the build checks the weight shares vertex by vertex and the tests check the hierarchy. Until something rotates an added bone, the surface deforms as before (0.0035 mm measured). The undersuit bends smoothly across the added bones, while armor plates, seams and the head ride one added bone each, so they stay rigid instead of creasing.
- Living motion is a procedural, time-based layer on the ten-joint rig rather than authored clips. The gait phase advances with distance, not time, and swings the legs along the body-frame travel direction. Sideways, each foot steps out on its own side and closes back, so the legs never cross. The body is lowered by how much the bent legs shorten, measured through the model's own rotation and the pelvis, hip and knee transforms. That keeps the lower sole at ground height, including through turning rolls, but only its height. At every speed and direction the grounded foot still slides most of the body's travel, mainly because the still-swinging foot becomes the lowest before it lands. True foot planting with inverse kinematics against authored clips must fix that. The torso's lean is cancelled at the hips, so leaning does not move the feet. The gait follows a smoothed velocity, so walls and cleared input never snap the legs. The layer moves only the model; the anchor shared by physics and camera is untouched. Lift is not delayed for the crouch, because that would slow the controls. Instead the model holds the last grounded height while the anchor rises, then catches up as the legs extend; reduced motion skips that hold. Plant switches at a caught fall or a touchdown blend their height change out over .15 s. Follow-through springs are kicked by changes of velocity and ring out from rest, with the kick capped so even an instant stop spreads over several frames. Pausing freezes the pose and resuming restarts the layer. A fuller skeleton with original authored clips remains the route to production-quality animation.
- The hero-facing correction keeps the explorer's back toward the chase camera in every flight state. Body pitch follows the travel slope but is bounded near the view pitch, the same way body yaw is bounded near the view yaw, because the camera boom follows the view and a lagging body showed its chest in climbs. The lean and the body pitch that offsets it share one settled value; fading them separately turned the body edge-on when braking out of a climb. In flight the whole body rolls into turns (`src/world/suitRoll.ts`): the roll follows the lateral acceleration of the actual travel, gathered per physics step in `src/game/turnSweep.ts` as the heading's turn times the full speed along the body's heading, clamped to 45 m/s² per step so a one-step turn weighs the same at every frame rate, eased and soft-saturated to a peak of .8 rad with hero poses and .55 classic, faded in between 2 and 6 m/s, and it gives way to the landing flare and to touchdown. Only speed along the heading counts, so flying backward gives no roll (the brake clip reads instead). Turns the flight safety makes (softenBounds, FlightSafety.anticipate, the controller's wall slide) are not counted, and the sweep stays closed .2 s after the last one, so the body does not bank away from a facade or the district edge it slides along; a per-step gate alone was rejected because anticipate corrects a slide on alternate steps and the gate rectified that into a bank toward the wall. The full speed rather than the horizontal speed drives the signal and the fade so diving and climbing turns bank as readably as level ones. It turns the body about the line of sight to the chase camera, weighted by how directly the camera looks along the flight axis (t̂·ĉ; within the facing bounds it stays above .07, so its floor at 0 is only a guard), so it is facing-invariant: the back-to-camera bound holds by construction and a steep view does not turn it into a heading swing. A roll about the body's long axis was rejected because it turned the back edge-on to the camera. The camera never rolls. The hover side tilt stays and fades out as the roll fades in; the earlier long-axis bank term is removed. Reduced camera motion removes the roll, easing it out when switched on mid-turn. Circling while strafing banks into the curve, because the body keeps part of its heading along the travel: .319 rad (hero) at 13 m/s and .699 at surge or with the two-thumb move stick (.480 classic). Elbows flex forward and knees flex backward for the -Z-facing model; a previous sign inversion bent both toward the camera and made the character read as facing it.
- The reference-hero rebuild corrects the long torso, short legs and exaggerated features using generated character and facial references. The authoring file retains editable pieces and both packed references. Matched Blender/runtime pivots preserve the ten-joint motion contract. Skin/hair now use two small embedded atlases; the 850 KB export ceiling explicitly includes their decoding/storage cost. Geometry remains below 20,000 triangles and eight material batches. Concept art and renders of the exported playable model are labeled separately.

- The human-hero correction replaces the rigid panel assembly with a continuous weighted surface because disconnected rigid sections cannot provide natural shoulder/elbow/knee deformation. It preserves the ten pose controls, original source ownership and the -Z forward convention. Back anatomy must read differently from the face/chest. Body yaw is bounded relative to the shared camera heading to keep sharp turns from presenting the face to the chase camera.

- The earlier rigid reconnaissance prototype used contoured ceramic panels, a continuous visor and an integrated power spine. Explicit GLB articulation metadata and batching by part/material supported that version; the weighted human surface supersedes its visual design.
- Desktop defaults to trackpad-only click-to-cruise with pointer steering and bounded vertical scroll speed (3–34 m/s, restarting at 8 m/s). Clicking again brakes; a held drag while hovering looks without launching. Mouse capture is an explicit saved alternative. Both feed the existing motion and camera controllers.
- Trackpad cruise stops on scene exit, HUD entry, pause, resize, touch handover and zoom gestures. Control/Command wheel and Safari gesture events remain available to native browser zoom. Scroll handling is limited to the active scene; dialogs retain native scrolling.
- One-thumb flight remains the primary touch input. Release brakes to hover; the separate Surge button stays removed.
- A second scene contact selects left movement/right view automatically. Roles are assigned by horizontal position, then retained by pointer identity. Both anchors reset on handoff; releasing either thumb returns to neutral one-thumb control until the remaining contact slides. Ground takeoff requires movement, so looking with two neutral contacts does not launch the suit.
- Only the active flight surface owns multi-touch browser gestures. Native page pinch zoom remains available after pausing and in the guide. Cancellation, a third scene contact, pause and rotation clear movement; a third-contact interruption requires all contacts to lift before restarting.
- Suit and camera share Rapier's interpolated presentation anchor. Visual banking follows travel direction, independently of pointer-event sampling.
- This district supports exterior exploration. Building collision volumes follow the full lower shell and stepped broken roof; upper-story voids are not playable interiors.
- Collision safety combines advance shape casts, stopping-distance limits, contact-normal velocity correction, final kinematic sweeps and physical perimeter colliders. Assisted landing requires support, clearance and a clear approach.
- Invalid saved positions recover to a checked landing. The user confirmed Safari for the current phone playtest; Chrome remains supported.
- Suit blaster (phase 1, `codex/shooter`, see `docs/plans/2026-09-22-shooter.md`): Fire never forces aim, so the player keeps flying. Speed tiers come from the 30 deg/s tracking budget; hip fire clamps the flight target's magnitude to 13 m/s instead of scaling it, and ADS hover-strafes at 6 m/s. There is no rotational pull (it would steer the flight heading); friction is applied per steering device at look() time. Flash gates (3 per second) cover the muzzle and the drones. Every blend and spring snaps to exact rest, so an idle or disabled shooter leaves look, camera and pose bit-identical. Every shooter frame callback runs behind a fault guard that turns the blaster off and keeps flight running. Blaster audio uses its own AudioContext, separate from the wind.
- Arm cannon (2026-09-23, owner decision "always a cannon"; plan section "Arm cannon and shot choreography" in `docs/plans/2026-09-22-shooter.md`): while the blaster is on, the right hand is hidden (hand_r scaled to 1e-3) inside an original arm cannon (`public/models/arm-cannon.glb`, built by `scripts/arm_cannon/build.py`) parented rigidly to forearm_r in that bone's bind frame; `src/world/cannonContract.ts` is the one source of its geometry. The aim solve swings the shoulder until the cannon's own barrel line runs through the crosshair target (exact, one minimum arc about the shoulder), from a carry pose that already points the barrel within about 11° of the line on screen, so a press fires on its own frame with the barrel near the line. Recoil is visual only: the gameplay muzzle is published from the solved pose before any kick, and effects read a separate kicked muzzle from the cannon. One shot clock drives the cannon, the arm and the whole body (the arm recoil snaps on the shot frame, the body answers from 74 ms at the torso to 185 ms at the hover legs, under the aim solve so it never lifts the cannon late; the grounded brace starts only from a burst's second shot), with the per-shot FOV punches removed to stay within four visible channels per shot. The armpit flap is fixed only while the blaster is on, by runtime skin weights on the cloned suit (a harmonic arm-share solve); suit.glb and rig.py are unchanged, so blaster-off stays bit-identical to main, and a rig.py fix for the blaster-off flap awaits owner approval. The shooter budget grows to 15 draw calls and 14k triangles for the cannon's 3 meshes and 2,808 triangles.
